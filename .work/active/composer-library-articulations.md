## Outcome and evidence

In Libraries, a composer can expand an installed library, see its actual patches, and expand a patch to see only articulations supported by evidence for that patch. A search such as `legato cello` or `cello harmonic` returns the matching patch with its library and matching articulation context, regardless of word order. Microphone positions remain installation attributes, never visible articulation children.

Current Prism data is maker → library → `LibraryInstrument` in `CatalogOutline`; a Kontakt NKI filename establishes a patch label, while its proprietary contents are not decoded. The current SINE adapter joins collection, instrument, and mic paths to verify installed content, but does not read articulation relations. `LibraryInstrument` is persisted inside existing versioned catalog payloads and owns patch-local tag overrides through vendor ID or exact path. The demo has Kontakt fixtures; tests have a synthetic SINE catalog with mic rows only. Do not infer every articulation of a library from its product description or from an NKI's opaque bytes.

[Spitfire Chamber Strings](https://www.spitfireaudio.com/products/spitfire-chamber-strings) lists Celli legato and Long Harmonics among product techniques; its [manual](https://spitfire-www-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/manuals/SpitfireChamberStrings_UserManual.pdf) distinguishes curated multi-articulation Basic/Extended patches from individual-articulation patches. Those lists establish product capabilities, not the contents of every installed NKI. [Metropolis Ark 1 notes](https://orchestraltools.helpscoutdocs.com/article/371-metropolis-ark-1-notes) identify Wolfenstein Low Strings as an 8-celli/10-bass ensemble with Low Strings Legato and other articulations. It is not a solo cello patch, and that source does not establish harmonics for it.
